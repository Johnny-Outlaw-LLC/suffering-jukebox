01 / Make it yours

One song can lead to a whole discography.

Build a playlist

Sign in, explore artists or search for a song, and use Add to playlist from the song’s menu. Choose an existing playlist or create one, then keep adding tracks as you explore.

Open your playlist to arrange the songs and play it in order or shuffle. A collection can mix different artists and releases.

Add an artist you cannot find

Use Add Music or Import Music in Explore Artists to bring in an artist, release, or song using the available link and search import tools. Review the matches before adding them.

Catalog imports add artist and song information with playable source links. They do not upload a recording or claim an artist’s rights. If you are publishing your own music, use the artist upload process.

02 / Pass it on

Share a favorite. Start a room.

Use the share controls to send an artist, album, song, or playlist link. Your friends can open the same content and start listening.

Want a shared queue? Sign in, start a listening room, and invite friends with its link or QR code. The host controls playback and room settings; guests add their picks to the queue where allowed.

03 / Your listening, explained

See the patterns in what you play.

Everyone with an account can open their own listening dashboard. See artists, songs, albums, plays, listening time, and activity over time.

Open My Listening Data from your account menu, or go to My Data & Analytics. Filter by platform, dates, artists, or songs, and switch between plays and hours where duration data is available. Refresh for the latest recorded activity.

04 / Bring your history

Your past listening is part of the collection.

Upload Spotify and Google listening exports from the analytics page, review them, and bring those listening records into your personal stats.

Import Spotify listening history
  1. Request Spotify’s Extended Streaming History export.

    Download the export when Spotify makes it available, then unzip it.

  2. Open My Data & Analytics → Import listening history.

    Choose Spotify, then select the streaming-history JSON files from the export. Include all the relevant JSON files for the period you want to bring in.

  3. Review and import.

    Check the preview before confirming. The import adds listening records to your private history. Reimported events are deduplicated, and IP addresses are stripped before storage.

Import Google / YouTube Music listening history
  1. Export your history with Google Takeout.

    Choose YouTube and YouTube Music, include history, and download and unzip the export. Find watch-history.html in its history folder.

  2. Open Import listening history → YouTube Takeout.

    Select the watch-history file. The importer identifies likely music locally and presents a preview; uncertain entries start unchecked so you can review them.

  3. Choose the music entries and confirm.

    Check the selection, then import it into your personal history. Takeout does not include listening duration, so these records contribute plays rather than invented playback hours.

Listening history is a record of what you played. Importing it adds stats, rather than audio files or a playable collection. Use the next step to find music that is missing from the catalog.
Add missing music from the analytics page
  1. Find a missing artist or song in your imported history.

    Select Add to Jukebox beside a missing entry, without leaving analytics.

  2. Choose songs and find matches.

    Select the songs you want, then choose Find matches to search YouTube for playable sources.

  3. Check the matches before adding.

    Review the source videos and correct artist or song names where needed. Confirm the selected matches with Add songs. They become catalog entries you can explore and add to playlists.

A source-video match is separate from a personal audio upload. It does not automatically enable background play or CarPlay.

05 / Keep it playing

Screen off. Music on.

Approved artist-uploaded music is already available for background play and CarPlay. For other catalog songs, you can upload your own audio files for private playback.

Upload your own background-play content
  1. Sign in and open Settings → Audio Storage.

    You can also use Enable Background Play in playlist exploration or an artist/album’s audio controls.

  2. Select your audio files and review the matches.

    Match the files to the correct artist, album, and songs. Use the upload screen’s current storage allowance and check the track assignments before uploading.

  3. Play the uploaded audio in the mobile app.

    Your personal files stay private to your account. They can play with the screen locked or the app in the background, and stream in Apple CarPlay through the iPhone app. Save an offline copy on the phone if you want playback without a connection.

Use the Background Enabled filter to find eligible content. A playlist is eligible as a whole only when every song has usable audio; a YouTube-only song in a mixed queue cannot keep playing in the background.

Artist audio

Approved recordings uploaded by artists are available to listeners by default. You do not need a personal copy.

Your uploads

Audio you add through your personal storage is for your account, rather than public artist distribution.

Apple CarPlay

Connect the iPhone app to a compatible car. Eligible audio streams directly; downloading first is optional.
