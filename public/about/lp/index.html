About Listening Party

Listening Party is a free online playlist player at listeningparty.stream. Build a playlist from anything on YouTube, share it with a link, read the words as they play, and listen together in a room. Nobody has to sign in to listen.

Start listening Read the guide

What it is

Most music apps are built around a library. Listening Party is built around the playlist, and around the people you play it for. Open a playlist and press play. Make your own from songs you find on YouTube. Send the link to a friend, or put a room on air and let everybody in it add songs from their own phone.

There is nothing to install and nothing to pay for. An account only matters when you want the site to remember things: your playlists, your ratings, your play history, and the rooms you host.

What you can do here

  • Browse public playlists, starting with three picks on the home page: the newest, the most watched, and the one played most here lately
  • Play anything with a queue you can reorder, search and save as a playlist
  • Build a playlist song by song, or pull songs in from a YouTube playlist link
  • Read lyrics that light up line by line as the song plays, and fix them when they are wrong
  • Share any public playlist with a link that opens straight into the player
  • Host a room: guests scan a code, join with no sign-in, and add to the queue you are playing
  • See your own listening history, charted

The guide explains all of it.

Listening together

A room is your player seen from the outside. Everyone in it gets the same song at the same second, with the same line of the words lit up, and the songs guests pick land in your queue with their name beside them. You stay in charge: guests can add songs and take back their own, and you can reorder, skip, remove and ban.

How it works, and what it is not

Everything you hear is a public YouTube video. Listening Party does not host music. When you press play you are watching a YouTube video, and the artist and label get that view exactly as they would if you had opened it on YouTube yourself. The one exception is background play on a phone with the screen off, which uses an audio file you uploaded for your own account only.

Song titles, artwork and lyrics come from public music databases, from whichever YouTube upload could be found, and from listeners. Some of it will be wrong, and all of it can be fixed from inside the app.

Listening Party is for personal listening and for playing music with people you know. It is not built or licensed for commercial use or public performance.

A sister site

Listening Party runs on the same engine as Suffering Jukebox, which leads with artists and whole discographies instead of playlists. The two share one catalogue and one account, so a playlist you make on either site is there on both.
