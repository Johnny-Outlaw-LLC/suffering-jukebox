About Suffering Jukebox

A free online jukebox anyone can add to. It started as a Silver Jews stream and a Purple Mountains player for David Berman fans.

What Suffering Jukebox is

Suffering Jukebox is a jukebox that listeners build. Every song plays from a public YouTube video, so there is nothing to install, nothing to pay for, and no account needed to press play. What the site adds is the shape around the music: discographies laid out on a timeline, charts of what gets listened to, lyrics you can read and fix, playlists, and a way to put your collection on air so a room full of people can queue songs from their phones.

It began as one artist's catalogue and now holds music from more than 170 artists, because anyone signed in can bring in a song, a playlist, or a band's whole studio discography, and choose whether it joins the shared catalogue or stays private to them.

Where it started

The site was built for the music of David Berman: the Silver Jews discography and the Purple Mountains album in one place, so you could stream songs, explore lyrics, rate tracks and build playlists without digging through scattered links.

The name comes from “Suffering Jukebox,” a song on Silver Jews’ final studio album Lookout Mountain, Lookout Sea (2008). This site is a fan-built tribute, not affiliated with Drag City or the Berman estate.

Purple Mountains

David Berman returned in 2019 with Purple Mountains, his first release after retiring Silver Jews. The self-titled album is included in Suffering Jukebox alongside the full Silver Jews catalog.

What you can do here

  • Browse a discography by year, on a timeline, by views, or as one sortable table of songs
  • Chart albums by YouTube views, by plays on this site, or by how listeners rated them
  • Play anything with a queue you can reorder, search and save as a playlist
  • Read lyrics that scroll in time with the song, and correct them when they are wrong
  • Add a song, a playlist, or a whole discography, publicly or privately
  • Put your jukebox on air so guests can queue songs from their phones with no sign-in
  • Share a link to any jukebox, or download a picture of its charts
  • See your own listening history, charted, and export it

How it works, and what it is not

Everything you hear is a public YouTube video. Suffering Jukebox does not host music. When you press play you are watching a YouTube video, and the artist and label get that view exactly as they would if you had opened it on YouTube yourself. The one exception is background play on a phone with the screen off, which uses an audio file you uploaded for your own account only.

Albums, dates, artwork, tracklists and lyrics come from public music databases and from whichever YouTube upload could be found, and are edited by listeners. Any of it can be wrong: a song can be matched to a live version, a record can be missing songs YouTube does not carry, and a release year can belong to a reissue. All of it is fixable from inside the app, but nothing here should be treated as a discography of record. There is more on that in the guide.
